## Vocal arrangement

- **Lead:** single female voice throughout — warm, expressive, capable of both intimate vulnerability and emotional power
- **No backing vocals** — this song belongs to one voice alone
- **Verse 1 & Pre-Chorus 1:** almost a whisper — intimacy is everything here
- **Chorus 1:** voice can open up slightly but remains controlled — the absence of drums keeps it fragile
- **Chorus 2:** more confident as drums enter softly underneath
- **Bridge:** full emotional release — the singer can let everything out here
- **Final Chorus:** maximum power, but never losing the emotional core
- **Outro:** whispered, almost spoken — mirror of how it began

---

## Piano

- The intro melody must be the same melody that returns in the chorus — this creates a sense of recognition and loss when it comes back with lyrics
- Touch: gentle, not too much pedal — clear and melancholic
- Throughout the song the piano never stops — it is the emotional spine of the track
- In the outro: return to exactly the intro melody, played alone, then fade to silence

---

## Accordion

- Enters in Verse 2 — soft, almost hesitant
- Should feel like a second breath joining the piano, not a new instrument arriving
- Not the upbeat festival accordion of the other tracks — this is slower, more sustained, melancholic
- Stays present through bridge and final chorus but never dominates over the vocal

---

## Acoustic guitar solo

- 8 bars, positioned after Chorus 2
- Plays the chorus melody — this is not a showcase solo, it is an emotional restatement
- Tone: warm, fingerpicked or light pick, no distortion, no effects
- Reference: the guitar feel of Sieben Brücken musst du geh'n
- Drums: brush snare only, or no drums at all — keep it fragile
- Piano and light accordion support underneath

---

## Drums

- Absent through Verse 1, Pre-Chorus 1, and Chorus 1 — this is deliberate and essential
- First entry in Chorus 2: soft brushes on snare, gentle kick — like a heartbeat starting again
- Guitar solo: brushes only
- Bridge: full drums enter — this is the emotional release moment
- Final Chorus: full drums, but never heavy — this is a ballad, not a stamper

---

## Strings

- Enter quietly in Chorus 1 — pads underneath, no melody
- Return in bridge and final chorus
- Should feel like warmth arriving, not drama

---

## Key change

Final Chorus modulates up +1 semitone (C minor → C# minor). The vocal line rises with it — this should feel like the last wave of emotion breaking.

---

## Outro

The outro mirrors the intro exactly — solo piano, same melody, no voice, no other instruments. The song ends where it began, but everything has changed. Fade to complete silence, not a fade-out.

---

## Emotional arc

| Phase | Feel |
|-------|------|
| Intro + Verse 1 | Alone, quiet, the morning after |
| Chorus 1 | Grief without drama — the weight of it |
| Verse 2 | Memory, photographs, searching |
| Chorus 2 | The grief is still there but breathing again |
| Guitar solo | Wordless — what cannot be said |
| Bridge | Breaking open — the wish, the impossibility |
| Final Chorus | Full release — everything out |
| Outro | Return to silence — acceptance |

## Notes

- The first chorus without drums is the most important production decision in the track — do not add drums early
- The guitar solo must play the chorus melody, not improvise freely — recognition is the point
- The outro silence after the piano fades should be a full 2–3 seconds of complete silence before the track ends
- Reference track for overall feel and pacing: Sieben Brücken musst du geh'n (Truck Stop)
- This is the emotional anchor of the album — place it in the second half of the tracklist
